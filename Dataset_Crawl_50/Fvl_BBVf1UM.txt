Diet can significantly affect psoriasis. While not a direct cause, a diet rich in anti-inflammatory substances like Omega-3 fatty acids found in salmon, mackerel, flaxseed, fruits, and vegetables can help reduce inflammation, improve symptoms, and support skin health. Conversely, foods like fried foods, fast food, refined sugar, alcohol, and red meat can increase inflammatory responses, making the condition worse. Furthermore, weight management through a healthy diet is crucial, as high cholesterol is a risk factor for psoriasis flare-ups and progression. Therefore, a healthy diet not only helps control the disease but also improves the quality of life for sufferers. Diet affects psoriasis because it is a risk factor that can determine whether the disease improves or worsens. Those who frequently eat spicy foods, consume alcohol, or even smoke cigarettes will experience increased metabolism. Simultaneously, it makes our skin more prone to dehydration, causing it to become drier. When psoriasis already affects the skin, an unhealthy diet can disrupt metabolism and increase sebum secretion, leading to increased water loss through the skin. This weakens the skin's natural defenses, resulting in red patches, thickened skin, and more scaling compared to normal individuals. To overcome this, we must adopt a healthy lifestyle and diet. We should eat plenty of green vegetables and fiber, and supplement with foods rich in zinc, vitamin C, and vitamin A – antioxidants that are good for the skin and body. Examples include guava, avocado, and salmon, which help restore and increase Omega-3 levels in the body and skin. Furthermore, it's crucial to drink enough water, especially for patients whose skin is damaged and has thick, red patches. With such flaky skin, almost all of our skin will become dry, so we need to replenish enough water to enhance microcirculation and provide essential nutrients to the skin. According to doctors, stress and anxiety can seriously affect psoriasis because they are triggers that worsen symptoms. Prolonged stress increases the production of hormones like cortisol, causing a strong inflammatory response in the body, thereby promoting the formation and spread of psoriasis plaques. In addition, stress weakens the immune system, making the body more susceptible to infections or external factors, making the disease harder to control. The vicious cycle between stress and psoriasis can also seriously affect the patient's psychology, leading to anxiety, depression, and low self-esteem, making treatment more complicated. Therefore, managing stress through exercise, meditation, or relaxation techniques is an important part of controlling psoriasis. Often, people with psoriasis already feel self-conscious and insecure, and this feeling of inferiority can further increase stress. However, it's important to note that stress can also worsen psoriasis. Stress, whether from work, family problems, or other personal issues, can trigger inflammatory responses, leading to a systemic inflammatory cascade that affects the skin, causing thickening, scaling, and ultimately, worsening psoriasis. This is a reciprocal interaction between the two conditions: neurological factors and psychological stress, which in turn affect the skin, such as psoriasis. These factors reinforce each other.
