What is psoriasis? Psoriasis is one of the common dermatological diseases caused by cell proliferation, forming long, red patches with white scales or lesions. Psoriasis is divided into five types: plaque psoriasis, erythrodermic psoriasis, blind psoriasis, guttate psoriasis, and psoriatic arthritis. Therefore, patients need to see a doctor to know exactly which type of psoriasis they have and find a suitable treatment method. Some of the main causes of psoriasis to date: The exact cause of psoriasis is still not definitively determined. However, some studies have shown that the disease may originate from several causes, such as genetic factors. Studies show that if someone in your family has psoriasis, the risk of developing the disease is very high. It is estimated that about 75 percent of psoriasis cases are due to genetic inheritance, and this factor is decisive. Decreased mental health, fatigue, stress, and low spirits cause the body to lose its immune balance. This is the most opportune time for bacteria to invade the body. Severe psoriasis can be caused by improper use of medication, which can lead to many adverse side effects and negatively impact health, causing psoriasis in many people. In addition, exposure to toxic substances, prolonged sun exposure, and substance abuse are also major causes of psoriasis. The most common symptoms of psoriasis vary depending on the type of psoriasis. Common symptoms include the appearance of red and white patches on the skin of the elbows, knees, head, and back. These patches are often covered by a white or slightly silvery layer that is easily visible. Patients also experience itching and discomfort due to inflammation and pain. The skin may also thicken and become rough due to the degeneration and stacking of cells, forming long scales. Furthermore, the skin may become infected and develop pus, causing pain and even serious complications. The presence of blood on the skin, along with the uncomfortable itching sensation associated with psoriasis, causes many people to scratch their skin, creating favorable conditions for bacteria to invade and further damage to other areas of the skin, such as the scalp and throughout the body. Nail damage is also a typical symptom of psoriasis; patients should pay attention to the appearance of small pits or vertical lines on their fingernails or toenails, which are brittle and thick in the free area. How do complications of psoriasis affect health? Besides the pain, itching, and discomfort caused by the disease, psoriasis also causes many people to feel self-conscious, embarrassed, and reluctant to interact with others. Over time, this can lead to depression due to isolation and social distancing. It can also cause metabolic disorders; in severe cases of psoriasis, the rate of metabolic complications is very high. Patients may experience hypertension, obesity, and impaired lipid metabolism, leading to arthritis. According to statistics, approximately... 42 percent of psoriasis patients suffer from arthritis, causing them pain. Furthermore, if left untreated for a long time, it can lead to paralysis and other serious health problems, including diabetes, kidney failure, cardiovascular issues, and even stroke at any time.
