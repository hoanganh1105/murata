Ladies and gentlemen, psoriasis can start early, between the ages of 16 and 22, or late, between the ages of 50 and 60. The disease can last a lifetime or have sudden, isolated flare-ups. The symptoms are very diverse, ranging from mild, unnoticed cases to severe cases that significantly impact daily life. This female patient, who has lived with psoriasis for nearly 40 years, has experienced two strokes, leaving her unable to speak clearly. She also has a lot of itching, a rash all over her body, peeling skin, and painful joints. She sought out traditional remedies. As her condition worsened, she heard a man say, "Buy a large container and soak the herbs in it for 30 minutes, then let her bathe, dry her, and then dress her. Only then did the medicine penetrate deeply and the condition worsened." In both cases, the psoriasis had severe complications; one suffered a stroke, the other... Bone and joint deformities; some studies show that people with psoriasis have three times the number of heart attacks compared to the general population; the rate of hypertension in people with psoriasis is 20%, and in those with severe psoriasis it is 47%; therefore, stroke is the most worrying complication for people with psoriasis, leading to loss of health and becoming a burden on families and society.
