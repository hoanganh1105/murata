I couldn't find work for over forty-one years. Oh my God, people gossiped so much. Many people knew, but they acted normal. Those who didn't know looked at me like I was some kind of monster. At that time, they told me to drink alcohol to die, but I didn't. Two or three years later, I gave up completely. The shared experiences of patients above reflect the feelings of many people who have psoriasis. Most of them feel ashamed and insecure, making it difficult to find work. More seriously, many suffer from depression. This can cause psoriasis to flare up more severely. People fear that this disease is contagious, and this causes feelings of shame and insecurity in patients. Conversely, when patients experience psychological effects, stress, and anxiety, it can cause flare-ups or worsen psoriasis. According to doctors, psoriasis is most common in people aged 15 to 35, with quite diverse manifestations ranging from chronic psoriasis, guttate psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, to severe forms such as generalized pustular psoriasis and erythrodermic psoriasis. Although this is a disease that may not be completely curable, there are now many methods to treat it and completely clear the skin. We can help patients clear the lesions and return to their normal lives. However, it is important to note that these treatments require a long period of time, not just a day or two. While psoriasis may look frightening, this disease is not contagious through direct or indirect contact. To treat it, patients need to go to medical facilities with dermatology specialists and use medication as prescribed by a doctor. Do not self-medicate, especially with drugs of unknown origin, as this can worsen psoriasis and lead to generalized erythrodermic psoriasis.
